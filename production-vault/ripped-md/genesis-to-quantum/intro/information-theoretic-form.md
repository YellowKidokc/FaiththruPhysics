Sections 1 and 2 give us two domains — one where all states coexist, one where they resolve sequentially. These are not contradictions. They are different projections of the same joint distribution, valid under their own algebras. Both true. Neither complete alone.

The substrate requires a generator, a state, and a relation between them. Two components cannot close over transformations — the system leaks. Three is the minimum for self-consistent evolution. This is a structural claim about closure, not a theological assertion.

Information extracted is bounded by the interaction, not by the observer's awareness. This constrains all claims about "consciousness causes collapse."

Phase III — Interpretation + Regimes

8
Delayed Choice (Information Ordering)

If the fundamental frame (Section 3) holds all states co-present, then a choice made "later" in the derivative frame can condition statistics "earlier" — without violating causality. This is not backward causation. It is an update of the joint distribution across frames. The dual-frame structure predicted this.

ExperimentChoice at $t_2$ conditions statistics at $t_1$
Formal$p(y \mid \text{choice}_{t_2}) \neq p(y)$ marginally
ConstraintNo signal to past  *no causal violation*
InterpretUpdate of joint distribution, not backward causation

9
Regimes (Same System, Different Constraints)

The coupling architecture $C$ is not fixed. Different values of $C$ produce different observable behavior from the same underlying system. This explains why the same physics appears to operate differently across regimes — the substrates didn't change, the coupling did. One system. Multiple expressions.

States$C_0$ : sparse, mediated, fragile  ·  $C_1$ : distributed, direct, stable
DynamicsSame $\Sigma_F$, $\Sigma_D$  —  different $C$ ⇒ different observables
ConclusionOne system, regime-dependent behavior

Coupling-Architecture Modification

Sections 1–9 describe the architecture. This section describes the repair. If the substrate broke through symmetry-breaking (Section 5), then restoration requires the reverse sequence — localization into the broken phase, coherent stabilization under its constraints, release of accumulated energy, and irreversible update of the coupling constant. Five steps. Each one necessary. Skip any step and the architecture remains broken.

Step 1Localization:  $\Psi_F \to \psi_D$  *fundamental state enters derivative space (SSB)*
WhyThe fix must operate inside the broken domain. Cannot repair $\Sigma_D$ from outside $\Sigma_D$.

Step 2Stabilization:  $t \geq \tau_{\text{lock}}$  *coherence maintained under decoherence pressure*
WhyTransient coupling is insufficient. $\psi_D$ must persist long enough to satisfy the coherence constraint. Duration is not arbitrary — it is determined by the system.

Step 3Release:  $\psi_D \to \Psi_F$  *latent energy $\Delta E$ discharged at transition*
WhyStabilization accumulates potential across the substrate boundary. Release converts it into the coupling update.

Step 4Update:  $C_0 \to C_1$  *coupling architecture permanently modified*
WhyThis is the point of irreversibility. The old coupling constant is replaced. The system cannot return to $C_0$.

Step 5Propagation:  $C_1 \otimes \Sigma_D$  *new coupling available across derivative space*
WhyThe update is not local. $C_1$ propagates to all entangled subsystems in $\Sigma_D$. Activation still requires $Q = 1$ (Section χ).

Irreversiblecost $\to \infty$ in volume limit
