# The Bilateral Audit — Theophysics Research Initiative

The John Templeton Foundation has spent fifty years asking how science and religion can be in genuine dialogue. The papers are good. The dialogue is thin. The gap has not closed. **Here is why: every attempt starts from a broken premise.**

The Arrangement

## The 80/20 Split Nobody Voted For

Science owns eighty percent of the public room. Logic. Mathematics. Prediction. Technology. Medicine. Policy. The definition of what counts as knowledge. Christianity owns the other twenty. Sunday morning. Personal devotion. Private morality. The polite acknowledgment that faith is meaningful to those who hold it — as long as it stays in its lane.

This arrangement feels earned. It feels like the verdict of four centuries of scientific progress over religious dogma. It is not. It is a verdict rendered by one party after it quietly took the other party's foundation and did not acknowledge it.

The 80% science owns only works because the 20% is true. That is the structural contradiction nobody names.

Before science takes its first measurement, it must borrow ten things it cannot prove with its own method. That the external world exists. That it is orderly and knowable. That logic works. That human cognition reliably tracks reality. That mathematics — an abstract invention — actually describes physical structure. That nature behaves consistently across time. That truth exists. That honesty matters. That simpler explanations are better. That the universe is comprehensible to minds at all.

Ten assumptions. Zero provable by experiment. Every one required before the experiment can be designed. These assumptions do not describe a neutral universe. They describe a universe that is rational, ordered, knowable, and accessible to minds. They describe a universe that theology has been describing for three thousand years. Science did not build that foundation. It borrowed it. Then it used the borrowed foundation to declare the lender out of bounds.

That is not a settlement. That is a structural contradiction. And it is the reason fifty years of Templeton funding has produced dialogue without resolution. You cannot have genuine dialogue between two parties when one party is operating on premises it took from the other without acknowledgment.

The Method

## Hold Both Sides to the Same Standard

The standard move in science-religion discourse is to ask whether religious claims survive scientific scrutiny. That is one direction. It is half an audit. The bilateral audit runs the other half too.

Hold God accountable to science's own standards — falsifiability, predictive power, explanatory scope, internal consistency, reproducibility. Run the full pipeline. Do not protect theological claims from pressure.

Then hold science accountable to the same standard. Can it ground its own axioms? Can it explain consciousness — not map its neural correlates, but explain why there is something it is like to be a brain? Can it explain why abstract mathematics describes physical reality with precision that borders on the absurd? Can it account for the fine-tuning of cosmological constants? Can it explain where information comes from, not how it is processed once it exists?
