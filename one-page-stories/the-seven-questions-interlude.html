The Seven Questions

Every claim is a tree with roots. The method asks whether the roots can actually hold.

If "mass-energy curves spacetime" is true, what else must be true underneath it?

◆

A claim is not just a sentence. It has assumptions, definitions, mechanisms, tests, limits, consequences, and failure conditions. Most arguments hide those layers.

The 7Q method refuses to hide them. It turns a claim around until every load-bearing surface is visible.

That is why it works across domains. Physics claims, theological claims, social claims, and method claims all have roots.

The goal is not to make every claim sound smart. The goal is to make every claim inspectable.

Proof by Classification

When the seven questions are asked in order, the reader stops seeing an argument as a blur. The claim becomes a machine with parts.

Each question classifies a different layer: what is being claimed, what it assumes, what it produces, what would break it, and what survives inspection.

01

Name the claim.

02

Expose the assumptions.

03

Define the mechanism.

04

Identify the tests and kill conditions.

05

Let the surviving structure earn its identity.
