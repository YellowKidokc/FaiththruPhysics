## How this fits the pipeline

What/How/Why is the ROUTER — which jurisdiction owns this claim?
These 24 concepts are the TEST BATTERY — how we evaluate claims within each jurisdiction.

### WHAT claims get tested with:
- Proposition (is it a real claim?)
- Quantifiers (all/some/none — is scope stated?)
- Evidence (induction — what observations support it?)
- Falsifiability (what would count against it?)
- Categories (is it the right kind of evidence?)

### HOW claims get tested with:
- Validity (does conclusion follow from premises?)
- Soundness (are premises true AND logic valid?)
- Implication (if P then Q — is the chain real?)
- Necessary/sufficient conditions (is this required or just enough?)
- Consistency (does it contradict itself?)
- Deduction (does it follow necessarily?)
- Model fit (does the structure explain and predict?)

### WHY claims get tested with:
- Axioms (are starting assumptions stated honestly?)
- Abduction (is this the BEST explanation or just A explanation?)
- Category discipline (semantic ≠ metaphysical ≠ mathematical ≠ personal)
- Coherence (do the parts fit without contradiction?)
- Identity (are terms stable through the argument?)
- Proof vs evidence vs pattern (is the claim level honest?)

## The 24 Concepts


1. Proposition — a claim that can be true or false
2. Premise — a starting claim that bears load
3. Conclusion — what follows from premises
4. Inference — the move from premise to conclusion
5. Validity — conclusion follows from premises (structure)
6. Soundness — valid + true premises (the real target)
7. Consistency — no self-contradiction
8. Contradiction — A and not-A both asserted
9. Identity — A = A, terms don't shift mid-argument
10. Excluded middle — either A or not-A
11. Implication — if P then Q
12. Necessary condition — required but not sufficient
13. Sufficient condition — enough to guarantee
14. Quantifiers — all/some/none/most (scope)
15. Categories — don't treat one kind of thing like another
16. Equivalence — two claims mean the same thing logically
17. Deduction — general rule to necessary conclusion
18. Induction — repeated observation to probable conclusion
19. Abduction — inference to best explanation
20. Coherence — parts fit together without contradiction
21. Falsifiability — what would count against this?
22. Proof — chain where each step follows by accepted rules
23. Axiom — starting assumption, stated honestly
24. Model — structured representation, tested by explanation and prediction

## The four don'ts (from GPT)

- Do not confuse pattern with proof
- Do not confuse semantic structure with metaphysical conclusion
- Do not confuse coherence with certainty
- Do not confuse interpretation with measurement

## The balancing truth

But do not throw the pattern away.
A measured pattern can be real evidence.
It just has to be placed at the correct logical level.
