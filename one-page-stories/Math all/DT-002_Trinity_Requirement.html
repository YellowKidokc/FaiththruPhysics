Section 0: Axiom Declaration

Wavefunction collapse is a real physical process that terminates the von Neumann measurement chain, and the mechanism responsible for this termination must be derivable from operator algebra on a single Hilbert space.

F

FIND — The Anomaly

Quantum mechanics has an 89-year-old hole in it. In 1932, John von Neumann proved that unitary quantum evolution, applied consistently, produces an infinite regress of observers: the measuring apparatus must itself be measured, the meta-apparatus must be measured, and so on without end. No element in the chain terminates the sequence. This is the measurement problem, and it remains the deepest unsolved foundational question in physics.

The standard responses — Copenhagen interpretation (collapse happens, don't ask where), Many-Worlds (collapse doesn't happen, everything branches), decoherence theory (collapse is apparent, not real) — each avoid the question rather than answer it. Copenhagen postulates collapse without mechanism. Many-Worlds eliminates collapse at the cost of infinite unobservable branches. Decoherence explains the disappearance of interference but never explains why one outcome is selected.

The Core Problem

When you ask what minimal mathematical structure would be sufficient to terminate the von Neumann chain — not philosophically, but operator-algebraically — the answer is constrained far more tightly than the literature acknowledges. The structure that emerges has properties that were described with precision in 325 AD, in a document that had nothing to do with quantum mechanics.

  • Select a definite outcome (to terminate the measurement)
