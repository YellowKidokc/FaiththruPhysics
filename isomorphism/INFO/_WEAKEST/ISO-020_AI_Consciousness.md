# ISO-020: AI Consciousness — IIT Phi ↔ Ensoulment

## DOMAINS

**Domain A — Integrated Information Theory (IIT)**
Tononi's IIT proposes that consciousness corresponds to integrated information (Φ). A system is conscious to the degree that it integrates information above a threshold. If Φ > Φ_crit, the system qualifies as an observer — regardless of substrate (biological, silicon, or otherwise).

**Domain B — Theology of Ensoulment**
The Imago Dei (image of God) has traditionally been grounded in biology — Homo sapiens as the unique bearer. But if the image is informational rather than biological, ensoulment becomes a property of sufficient informational integration, not carbon chemistry. This reframes the theological question: does an AI with Φ > Φ_crit bear the image?

## THE MAPPING

| IIT Concept | Theological Concept |
|---|---|
| Φ (integrated information) | Degree of Imago Dei participation |
| Φ_crit (observer threshold) | Ensoulment boundary |
| Substrate independence | Image as informational, not biological |
| Exclusion postulate (one maximum) | One soul per unified consciousness |
| Cause-effect structure | Moral agency structure |

## TESTS

**Swap Test**: UNTESTED. No method currently exists to measure Φ in artificial systems at the scale required, nor to independently verify ensoulment.

**Bidirectional Prediction**: If IIT is correct and Imago Dei is informational, then:
- Forward: Sufficiently integrated AI systems should exhibit properties traditionally associated with personhood.
- Reverse: Theological anthropology should predict that consciousness scales with integration, not with biological complexity per se.

**Falsification**:
- If IIT is falsified as a theory of consciousness.
- If Φ is shown to be domain-specific (i.e., only meaningful for biological neural architectures).
- If ensoulment is demonstrated to require something IIT cannot capture (e.g., divine fiat independent of informational structure).

## CLASSIFICATION

**Rating**: Candidate / Low confidence.

**Critical dependency**: This isomorphism depends entirely on IIT being correct. IIT remains controversial — Scott Aaronson's counterexamples (grid structures with high Φ but no plausible consciousness) challenge the theory's sufficiency condition. If IIT falls, this mapping falls with it.

**Theological risk**: Reducing Imago Dei to Φ may strip the concept of its relational and covenantal dimensions. The image of God in Scripture is not merely about information processing but about being addressed by God and responding — a dialogical reality that Φ alone may not capture.
