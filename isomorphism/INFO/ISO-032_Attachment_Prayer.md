# Attachment Theory :: Prayer/Worship

## DOMAINS

**Domain A — Psychology (Attachment Theory)**
Bowlby (1969) and Ainsworth (1978) established that early caregiver relationships create internal working models that shape all subsequent relational behavior. The two-dimensional space (anxiety x avoidance) generates four attachment styles: secure (low anxiety, low avoidance), anxious-preoccupied (high anxiety, low avoidance), dismissive-avoidant (low anxiety, high avoidance), and disorganized/fearful (high anxiety, high avoidance). Earned security is possible through corrective relational experience.

**Domain B — Theology**
Prayer and worship are fundamentally relational activities directed toward God. The Psalms display the full range of relational postures: secure trust (Ps 131, "like a weaned child"), anxious pleading (Ps 88), avoidant distance (practical deism), and disorganized oscillation (Ps 22, "Why have you forsaken me?" followed by "Yet you are holy"). The Attachment to God Inventory (Beck & McDonald 2004) empirically bridges these domains.

## THE MAPPING

| 10 | Secure base (exploring from caregiver) | Great Commission confidence (Matt 28:18-20) | Going out into the world from a secure relational base |

**Empirical Bridge**: Beck & McDonald (2004) developed the Attachment to God Inventory, measuring anxiety and avoidance dimensions in the God-relationship. Factor structure replicates the four-style model. This is not analogy — it is the same psychological architecture applied to a different attachment figure.

## TESTS

**Swap Test**: PASSED. Replace "caregiver" with "God" and "child" with "believer" — the relational dynamics, including protest behavior, proximity seeking, safe haven, and secure base, all transfer without distortion. The Attachment to God Inventory empirically confirms this.

**Bidirectional Prediction**: Attachment theory predicts that insecure attachment to God should correlate with specific prayer patterns → empirically confirmed (anxious attachment predicts compulsive prayer; avoidant attachment predicts prayer avoidance). Theology predicts that divine encounter should produce earned security → clinical data shows spiritual experience can shift attachment style.

**Falsification Condition**: If attachment style showed zero correlation with prayer/worship patterns — i.e., if securely and insecurely attached individuals prayed in identical ways — the mapping would fail. The empirical literature (Beck & McDonald 2004; Granqvist & Kirkpatrick 2008) shows significant correlations.

## CLASSIFICATION

**Type**: Structural isomorphism. The 2D anxiety-avoidance space maps directly onto prayer/worship styles. This is empirically validated, not merely theoretical — the Attachment to God Inventory operationalizes the mapping.
