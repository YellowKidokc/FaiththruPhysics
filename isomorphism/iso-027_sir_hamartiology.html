Domain A — Epidemiology

SIR Model · R₀ = β/γ · Threshold Dynamics

The SIR (Susceptible-Infected-Recovered) model is the foundational mathematical framework for disease propagation through a population. Its elegance is in its simplicity: three states, three differential equations, one decisive number — R₀.

SIR Differential Equations
dS/dt = -βSI dI/dt = βSI - γI dR/dt = γI

S = Susceptible (not yet infected), I = Infected (can transmit), R = Recovered (immune or removed). β = transmission rate (contacts × probability of transmission per contact). γ = recovery rate. The product βSI drives spread; γI drives resolution.

Basic Reproduction Number
R₀ = β/γ

R₀ is the average number of secondary infections produced by one infected individual in a fully susceptible population. R₀ > 1: epidemic (exponential spread). R₀ < 1: disease dies out. R₀ = 1: endemic equilibrium. The threshold is the most consequential number in public health.

Herd Immunity Threshold

The fraction of a population that must be immune to prevent epidemic spread: p_c = 1 - 1/R₀. For R₀ = 10 (measles), 90% immunity is required. For R₀ = 2, 50% suffices. Below this threshold, the infection cannot propagate — each case generates fewer than one new case on average. The righteous remnant as mathematical structure.

Domain B — Theology (Hamartiology)

Sin Propagation · Social Contagion · Jeroboam the Superspreader

Scripture treats sin as contagious — not merely individual moral failure but a socially propagating force. The language of disease, infection, and spread appears throughout the biblical witness.

A little leaven leavens the whole lump. 1 Corinthians 5:6 · Galatians 5:9
Do not be deceived: "Bad company ruins good morals." 1 Corinthians 15:33
See to it that no one fails to obtain the grace of God; that no "root of bitterness" springs up and causes trouble, and by it many become defiled. Hebrews 12:15

The theology explicitly models sin as network-propagating, contact-dependent, and threshold-sensitive. "A little leaven" does not spread to all eventually at any R₀ — at R₀ < 1, it dies out. The leavening claim asserts R₀ > 1 for unaddressed sin in a community.

Jeroboam Case Study

The Biblical Paradigm of the Theological Super-Spreader

Jeroboam son of Nebat — R₀ >> 1

The phrase "the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin" appears 21 times in 1–2 Kings. No other individual in the biblical record is associated with causing others to sin at this frequency or persistence. Every subsequent northern king is described as continuing in his sins — a transmission chain spanning two centuries.

What made Jeroboam a super-spreader rather than merely a sinner? He did not merely sin personally — he created infrastructure for transmission:

  • Set up golden calves at Dan and Bethel — alternative worship sites lowering the activation energy for idolatry (β↑)
  • Appointed non-Levitical priests — bypassed the legitimate transmission-disruption system (γ↓)
  • Changed the festival calendar — cultural normalization of the alternative system (β↑↑)
  • Made it a state policy — institutional rather than personal transmission

Epidemiologically: Jeroboam simultaneously raised β (lowered barriers to infection by normalizing the behavior and creating physical infrastructure) and lowered γ (removed recovery mechanisms by replacing the priesthood and changing the calendar). The result was maximum R₀ — not because he was uniquely evil but because he restructured the transmission system.

Application for Institutional Analysis

The Jeroboam model predicts that the most dangerous moral actors are not the most personally sinful individuals but those who restructure institutions to maximize transmission. Laws that normalize vice, platforms that algorithmically promote outrage, cultural products that lower resistance — these are infrastructure upgrades, not individual acts. The epidemiological lens identifies them as super-spreader interventions.

Bidirectional Predictions

Forward · Reverse · Cross-Domain Verification

Forward Prediction (Epidemiology → Theology)

SIR dynamics predict that sin propagation should follow contact-network topology, not random distribution. In a scale-free social network (see ISO-031), sin should concentrate around hubs. Empirically testable: apostasy should correlate with network centrality of the apostate, not merely with individual choice. Jeroboam's kingdom-wide effect is consistent with hub-node super-spreading in a scale-free network.

Reverse Prediction (Theology → Epidemiology)

The Judges cycle (sin → oppression → cry → deliverance → sin, repeated 6×) predicts epidemic oscillation — which SIR models with partial immunity produce as damped oscillations in endemic disease. The cycle's regularity is not narrative convenience; it is the expected output of a system with R₀ slightly above 1, incomplete immunity (spiritual), and stochastic reinfection.

Abraham Negotiation as Threshold Identification

Genesis 18:22-33: Abraham negotiates with God over Sodom, testing the herd immunity threshold. Would 50 righteous prevent destruction? 45? 30? 20? 10? This sequence is an explicit investigation of the critical threshold p_c. The negotiation ends at 10 — below which, the righteous remnant is insufficient to reach herd immunity in a city of Sodom's size. The theology independently arrives at the threshold concept.

06

Separator Tests & Falsification

Swap Test · Structural Robustness · Kill Condition

Swap Test — Passed
Replace "R₀" with "transmission rate of sin" and "quarantine" with "church discipline" — the structural predictions hold. Paul's explicit argument for church discipline in 1 Cor 5 uses leavening (R₀ > 1 logic) as the justification. The theology independently arrives at the epidemiological rationale without knowing the SIR model.
Jeroboam Prediction — Confirmed
The super-spreader model predicts that Jeroboam's effect should show exponential, not linear, propagation — because he did not merely sin but raised β and lowered γ for an entire kingdom. The biblical record confirms: 21 successive kings, two centuries, zero recovery. The prediction of sustained R₀ > 1 following infrastructure changes is confirmed.
Herd Immunity — Predicted and Attested
The Abraham negotiation (Gen 18) independently attests to herd immunity threshold logic: below a critical number of righteous, the community cannot be stabilized. This is not a metaphor. It is a functional claim about threshold dynamics in social groups — the same mathematics as SIR herd immunity.
Scope Caveat — Social vs. Personal Sin
The SIR mapping applies most precisely to socially propagating sin — sins that spread through contact, imitation, normalization. Private sins with no transmission mechanism (purely internal states) do not map onto SIR dynamics. The isomorphism is strongest for culturally and institutionally propagating sin patterns.
Falsification Condition

The mapping would break if:
(1) Sin propagation followed random rather than contact-network patterns — if isolated individuals were equally likely to adopt particular sins as densely connected ones.
(2) Moral collapse showed no threshold effects — if there were no critical mass below which righteous influence stabilizes a community.
(3) Church discipline showed no effect on community moral contagion — if isolation of unrepentant members did not reduce spread.

All three are empirically investigable and all three run counter to available data from sociology of religion and community studies.
