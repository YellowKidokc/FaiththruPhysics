01

The Claim

What is being asserted — and how it could be falsified
Structural Isomorphism · Operator Theory · Sola Gratia · Registry Entry ISO-012

The mathematics of Hermitian operators with binary spectra — specifically the commutation relation [σ, U] = 0 — is structurally isomorphic to the Reformed doctrine that moral orientation cannot be changed by self-generated effort. Define a Hermitian operator σ with eigenvalues ±1. The commutation relation states that unitary self-evolution U cannot change the eigenvalue of σ. The system may evolve, precess, rotate — but the sign stays fixed.

This is the mathematical content of sola gratia: salvation by works is impossible not as a cultural assertion but as a mathematical theorem. Self-generated evolution cannot flip a commuting eigenvalue. An external operator V with [σ, V] ≠ 0 is required. The theology names that operator "grace." The mathematics derives the requirement independently.

 Kill Condition

If sustained self-effort could permanently flip moral orientation without any external input — that is, if [σ, U] ≠ 0 were demonstrated in either domain — the isomorphism breaks. Any system where internal dynamics alone can cross the eigenvalue boundary falsifies the mapping. Note: behavioral improvement (Ψ rotating in Hilbert space) does not falsify it; only genuine eigenvalue flip by self-evolution would do so.

02

Domain A — Mathematics

The Hermitian sign operator and its commutation structure
Define a Hermitian operator σ on a two-dimensional Hilbert space with orthonormal basis {|+1⟩, |−1⟩}. This operator represents a binary observable with exactly two possible measurement outcomes.
Spectral Decomposition of the Sign Operator
σ = (+1)|+1⟩⟨+1| + (−1)|−1⟩⟨−1| Eigenvalue equation: σ|+1⟩ = +1 · |+1⟩ σ|−1⟩ = −1 · |−1⟩
The system occupies one of two eigenstates. No superposition of +1 and -1 eigenvalues exists upon measurement — the outcome is binary, with no neutral option.

Let U be the unitary time-evolution operator generated by the system's own Hamiltonian H: U = e−iHt/ℏ. The commutation relation:

The Central Commutation Relation
[σ, U] = σU − Uσ = 0
When σ and U commute, they share eigenstates. The eigenvalue of σ is therefore a conserved quantity under time evolution generated by H. Self-generated dynamics cannot change which eigenstate the system occupies — only which state within the eigenspace.
What [σ, U] = 0 Guarantees

If the system begins in eigenstate |−1⟩, no amount of self-generated unitary evolution can produce eigenstate |+1⟩. The state vector may acquire complex phases, may precess in Hilbert space, may produce altered measurement statistics on other observables — but ⟨σ⟩ remains fixed at −1. Moral effort (U acting on |−1⟩) produces real behavioral change without changing the underlying eigenvalue.

To flip the eigenvalue requires an external operator V that does not commute with σ:

Requirement for Eigenvalue Flip
V must satisfy: [σ, V] ≠ 0 Then: V|−1⟩ = |+1⟩ is possible V|+1⟩ = |−1⟩ is possible No such flip is possible for any V satisfying [σ, V] = 0
The non-commutativity of V with σ is the mathematical prerequisite for crossing the eigenvalue boundary. An operator that commutes with σ preserves eigenstates by definition.

Domain B — Theology

Moral orientation as binary eigenstate
None is righteous, no, not one; no one understands; no one seeks for God. All have turned aside; together they have become worthless; no one does good, not even one. Romans 3:10–12 (ESV)

The theological claim is that moral orientation — the fundamental alignment of a person toward or away from God — is binary. There is no neutral state. Matthew 12:30 states this explicitly: "Whoever is not with me is against me." The moral spectrum consists of exactly two eigenvalues: +1 (righteous, aligned toward God) and −1 (fallen, aligned away from God).

The Key Theological Mapping

The commutation relation [σ, U] = 0 maps onto the theological claim that works-righteousness is structurally impossible — not merely difficult or improbable, but formally excluded by the mathematics of commuting operators. No behavioral program (U) generated by the fallen self (the system's own Hamiltonian) can alter the moral eigenvalue.

John 3:3 provides the theological counterpart to the external operator requirement: "Unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God." The new birth is an operation from outside the system — it does not commute with the moral orientation operator because it is not generated by the system's own Hamiltonian.

Key Claim — Formal Statement

The mathematical derivation of sola gratia
Central Theorem

[σ, U] = 0 is the mathematical content of "salvation by works is impossible." This is not a metaphor. It is a theorem of operator algebra: an operator that commutes with the sign operator cannot change the sign eigenvalue. Self-generated evolution commutes with the sign. Therefore self-generated evolution cannot save.

Formal Derivation — Self-Evolution Cannot Save
σ_moral (U_self |humanity_fallen⟩) = U_self (σ_moral |humanity_fallen⟩) = U_self (−1 |humanity_fallen⟩) = −1 (U_self |humanity_fallen⟩)
Since [σ_moral, U_self] = 0, we can commute them. The eigenvalue (−1) factors out and survives any self-generated evolution. The state evolves; the sign does not change. QED: works cannot justify.
The Grace Operator — Eigenvalue Flip
V_grace |humanity_fallen⟩ = |humanity_redeemed⟩ where [σ_moral, V_grace] ≠ 0 This is possible only because V_grace is NOT generated by the human Hamiltonian H_human. It comes from outside.
The grace operator V_grace does not commute with the moral sign operator, which is precisely why it can change the eigenvalue. Externality is not a feature added to grace theology — it is a mathematical requirement of any operator capable of eigenvalue-flipping. See ISO-013 for the full grace operator formalism.
Connection to ISO-013

ISO-013 provides the explicit matrix form of V_grace (the idempotent projection G = [[1,1],[0,0]]) and proves the additional properties: G² = G (once is enough), G†G ≠ I (irreversible), rank-deficient (prior state erased). ISO-012 establishes why an external operator is necessary; ISO-013 specifies what properties that operator must have.

Separator Tests

Tests that distinguish isomorphism from analogy
Swap Test — PASSED
Replace "eigenvalue" with "moral orientation" and "unitary evolution" with "works of the law" — the operator algebraic structure holds in both directions. The commutation relation maps cleanly onto the theological claim without distortion in either direction. The mathematical result (eigenvalue invariance under commuting operator) is exactly what the theology describes (works cannot change moral nature).
Bidirectional Prediction — PASSED
Forward: operator theory predicts that behavioral improvement (U acting on |−1⟩) should produce measurable changes in secondary observables without changing the fundamental orientation. This matches the theological observation that moral effort produces real behavioral change while leaving the underlying condition unchanged. Reverse: the theological claim of binary moral states predicts that the mathematical model should require a binary-spectrum operator, not a continuous one.
Non-Interchangeability — PASSED
The sign operator is not the same as the grace operator (ISO-013). The grace operator is not the same as moral orientation. Moral orientation is not the grace operator. All three roles are structurally distinct and non-interchangeable in both domains.
 Falsification Condition

If any sustained self-generated behavioral program (without external intervention) produced a permanent, stable flip of fundamental moral orientation — not behavioral change, but eigenvalue change — the isomorphism would be falsified. The test distinguishes behavioral improvement (which the model predicts and accommodates) from genuine regeneration (which the model says requires an external operator). A documented case of self-bootstrapped, permanent moral-nature transformation — sustained across time without a grace-analog — would be the kill condition.
