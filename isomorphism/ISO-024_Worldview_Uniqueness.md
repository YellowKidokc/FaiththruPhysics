# ISO-024: Worldview Uniqueness — 8 Boundary Conditions ↔ 8/8 Christian Solution

## DOMAINS

**Domain A — Mathematics**
A system of 8 simultaneous constraints (boundary conditions) generically has a unique solution or no solution. The more independent constraints imposed, the more tightly the solution space is restricted. If a solution exists satisfying all 8, it is likely unique (assuming the constraints are independent and non-degenerate).

**Domain B — Theology**
Christianity claims to uniquely satisfy 8 worldview boundary conditions (as defined by the Theophysics framework): origin, meaning, morality, destiny, suffering, identity, community, and transcendence. No other worldview is claimed to satisfy all 8 simultaneously without internal contradiction.

## THE MAPPING

| Mathematical Concept | Theological Concept |
|---|---|
| Boundary condition | Worldview requirement |
| Simultaneous satisfaction | Internal consistency across all domains |
| Unique solution | Christianity as the only 8/8 worldview |
| Over-determined system | More constraints than free parameters → uniqueness |
| No solution exists | Worldview that fails one or more conditions |

## TESTS

**Swap Test**: FAILED. The mathematical structure (simultaneous constraints → unique solution) is generic and content-free. It does not predict which worldview satisfies the conditions — it only says that if 8 independent constraints are imposed, the solution is likely unique. The theological conclusion (Christianity = unique solution) is smuggled in via the choice of boundary conditions, not derived from the mathematical structure.

**Bidirectional Prediction**: None. The math predicts "at most one solution exists" but cannot identify it. The theology claims to be that solution but the claim is not generated by the math.

**Falsification**:
- If another worldview demonstrates 8/8 satisfaction independently (i.e., evaluated by someone without prior commitment to Christianity).
- If the 8 boundary conditions are shown to be non-independent (reducing the effective constraint count).
- If the conditions are shown to be defined in a way that only Christianity could satisfy them by construction.

## CLASSIFICATION

**Rating**: Candidate / Very low confidence.

**Circularity**: SEVERE. This is the central problem. The 8 boundary conditions were formulated within a Christian intellectual framework. Christianity was, as it were, in the room when the exam was written. The conditions may be implicitly tailored to produce the desired result. To escape this charge, the conditions would need to be:
1. Derived from a worldview-neutral source (e.g., cross-cultural anthropological universals).
2. Evaluated by assessors without prior commitment to the outcome.
3. Applied with the same rigor to all candidate worldviews.

None of these conditions have been met.

**What would raise confidence**: An independent, secular philosopher constructing equivalent boundary conditions and arriving at the same conclusion. Without that, this isomorphism functions as internal apologetics, not as a demonstration.
