The Claim

What is being asserted — and how it could be falsified
Structural Isomorphism · Math-Verified · Bidirectional · Canonical Registry Entry

The mathematical architecture governing gravitational attraction — Newton's inverse-square law, F = Gm₁m₂/r² — is structurally isomorphic to the dynamics of sin as described across theological traditions. Both phenomena describe an attractive force that increases with mass (moral weight) and decreases with the square of the distance from the attracting center. Both require an energy input exceeding the binding energy of the system in order to achieve escape. Both produce stable orbital patterns (habitual sin) and catastrophic collapse events (moral disintegration). The inverse-square law governs both domains: doubling the distance from the Source quarters the pull of sin.

This is not a metaphor. It is not an analogy. It is a structural correspondence — the same mathematical form appears in both domains because both are manifestations of the same underlying substrate (the POF 2828 chi-field, variables G and E) operating at different measurement scales. The claim is that if you understand orbital mechanics, you already understand the dynamics of habitual sin, conversion threshold effects, and moral collapse — because you are looking at the same equation from two different domains of measurement.

What Is NOT Claimed

Sin is not literally gravity. Moral mass is not physical mass. No assertion is made that the gravitational constant G and the moral binding constant Gₘₒₑₐₗ are numerically equal or measurable on the same instrument. The claim is purely structural: the same mathematical architecture governs both phenomena. Any test of this isomorphism must target structure, not substance.

 Kill Condition

Primary: If sin operated as a repulsive force — pushing moral agents away from disorder and toward the Source as a default tendency — the structural mapping breaks. The entire isomorphism depends on the attractive and accumulative nature of sin (parallel to gravitational attraction).

Secondary: If empirical studies of moral decay showed a linear relationship with distance from religious community or practice — rather than an inverse-square or stronger accelerating relationship — the specific inverse-square form of the mapping would fail, even if a weaker directional mapping survived.

Tertiary: If conversion experiences showed smooth, gradual transitions rather than threshold effects (analogous to escape velocity), the binding-energy model embedded in the isomorphism would be disconfirmed.
