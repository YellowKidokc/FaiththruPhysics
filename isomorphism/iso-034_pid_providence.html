Domain A — Control Theory

The PID controller — three-term feedback, stability, and the controller/plant distinction
The Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controller is the workhorse of industrial and engineering feedback systems, governing everything from thermostats to aircraft autopilots. Its three-term structure captures three fundamentally different modes of error correction: present, past, and future.
PID Controller Transfer Function (Laplace Domain)
C(s) = K_p + K_i/s + K_d·s Output: u(t) = K_p·e(t) + K_i·∫e(τ)dτ + K_d·de/dt where: e(t) = r(t) - y(t) [error = setpoint - measured output] r(t) = reference setpoint y(t) = measured plant output u(t) = controller output (input to plant)
The controller drives the plant G(s) to reduce the error signal e(t) toward zero. The controller output u(t) is the mechanism of control — it is NOT the plant; it acts ON the plant.

The three terms operate on fundamentally different time horizons:

  • Proportional term K_p · e(t): Responds to the present error. If you are off-target now, apply correction proportional to how far off you are. Fast but cannot eliminate steady-state error alone — a pure P controller always maintains residual offset.
  • Integral term K_i · ∫e(τ)dτ: Responds to the accumulated past error. Integrates all previous deviations and keeps applying correction until error is exactly zero. The only term that can eliminate steady-state error. Slower to act, but drives the system to perfect alignment over time.
  • Derivative term K_d · de/dt: Responds to the rate of change of error — anticipating where the error is heading. If error is small but growing rapidly, the derivative term applies anticipatory correction. Predictive rather than reactive.
Closed-Loop System Architecture
r(t) → [CONTROLLER C(s)] → u(t) → [PLANT G(s)] → y(t) ↑__________________________________| [feedback: e(t) = r(t) - y(t)]
Critical architectural fact: the controller C(s) and the plant G(s) are DISTINCT subsystems. The controller acts ON the plant via its input u(t). The plant retains its own transfer function G(s) — its own dynamics, its own characteristic response. The controller does not replace or eliminate the plant.
Stability Margins

Stability requires adequate gain margin (how much gain can increase before oscillation) and phase margin (how much delay the system can tolerate before going unstable). Well-tuned PID controllers balance correction speed (high K_p) against stability (which degrades with excessive gain). This tradeoff maps directly onto theological concepts in Section 3.

Domain B — Theology of Providence

Conscience, sanctification, and prophetic warning as three modes of divine governance
Providence is God's ongoing governance of creation — not merely creation and then abandonment (deism), but active, continuous steering. The PID isomorphism reveals that this governance operates through three structurally distinct channels that mirror the three controller terms.
In him we live and move and have our being. Acts 17:28 (ESV) — Providence as continuous, not punctuated

The three providential channels:

  • Conscience (K_p — present): Immediate moral perception of present deviation from the setpoint. "Their conscience bearing witness, and their conflicting thoughts accusing or even excusing them" (Romans 2:15). Conscience responds to current error in real time — proportional to the magnitude of the deviation.
  • Sanctification (K_i — accumulated past): The long-term, integral transformation of character. "He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion" (Philippians 1:6). Sanctification accumulates — each experience of conviction, repentance, and correction is integrated into the ongoing transformation. This is the only mode that ultimately eliminates remaining sin (steady-state error goes to zero).
  • Prophetic warning (K_d — rate of change): Anticipatory correction — warning of where current trajectory leads before the error fully develops. "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand" — spoken when the trajectory is headed wrong, not merely when it has already arrived. Responds to the derivative (rate of change) of deviation, not the deviation itself.
The Critical Theological Point

Providence does NOT override human agency. The PID architecture shows exactly why this is formally consistent: the controller C(s) acts on the input to the plant G_human(s). The plant retains its own transfer function — its own dynamics, its own response characteristics, its own freedom to respond or not respond to the controller signal. God steers the input; the human system responds according to its own nature. This is the engineering-precise resolution of the free will / sovereignty paradox.

The Free Will Resolution

The controller/plant distinction as engineering-precise answer to the sovereignty paradox

The free will / sovereignty problem has been framed as a paradox for two millennia: if God is sovereign over all things, how can humans be genuinely free? If humans are genuinely free, how can God be truly sovereign? Both seem to require eliminating the other.

The PID architecture dissolves this paradox by providing a third option that no pre-engineering theology could articulate precisely: the controller is not the plant.

A feedback controller governs a physical plant without substituting for it. The thermostat does not become the room. The autopilot does not replace the aircraft. The controller acts on the input to the plant — it shapes what the plant receives — but the plant's transfer function G(s) is entirely its own. Two systems with identical G(s) will respond identically to the same controller output; two systems with different G(s) will respond differently to the same input. The plant's response is the plant's own.

Controller/Plant Separation — Formal Statement
Closed-loop transfer function: T(s) = C(s)·G(s) / (1 + C(s)·G(s)) Controller C(s) and Plant G(s) remain DISTINCT. The total response T(s) is a function of both — neither eliminates the other. Providence (C_divine(s)) operates on the input to G_human(s). The human system responds according to its own transfer function — its own will, nature, and disposition.
Sovereignty means C_divine(s) is real and operative. Free will means G_human(s) is real and not replaced. Both are true simultaneously because they operate at different points in the loop.
What This Resolves

Calvinist concern: Is God truly sovereign? Yes — C_divine(s) is always present and effective in the loop. The setpoint r(t) = God's will is the reference signal. Providence is real and operative.

Arminian concern: Does God override human will? No — G_human(s) is never replaced. The human plant retains its own transfer function. Two people receiving identical providential input respond differently because they have different G(s).

The resolution: Both traditions are correct about their primary claim and wrong only in assuming the claims are mutually exclusive. The controller/plant architecture shows they are simultaneously true and formally compatible.

Ki as Sanctification — Why the Integral Alone Eliminates Sin

The only term that drives steady-state error to zero
The integral term K_i is theologically the most important of the three PID components. It is the only term capable of eliminating steady-state error — and therefore the only analog for the doctrine that remaining sin is progressively eliminated through sanctification.
Why K_p and K_d Alone Are Not Enough

A pure proportional controller (K_p only, K_i = 0) always maintains a residual steady-state error. This is provable: if error e = 0, the controller output u = K_p · 0 = 0, and zero output cannot maintain the system against a finite disturbance. Therefore e ≠ 0 at steady state — there must be some nonzero error to produce nonzero control action.

A pure derivative controller cannot produce steady-state correction at all — it responds only to change, not to persistent offset.

Only the integral term accumulates until the error is driven to exactly zero, then maintains that correction indefinitely. This is simul justus et peccator: justified (setpoint reached) but with K_p and K_d providing the immediate responses while K_i (sanctification) works across a lifetime to close the gap.

Steady-State Error Analysis
With K_i = 0 (no sanctification): e_ss = lim(t→∞) e(t) = 1/(1 + K_p·G(0)) ≠ 0 [nonzero remaining sin — simul justus et peccator] With K_i > 0 (sanctification active): e_ss = lim(s→0) s·E(s) = 0 [sin eliminated asymptotically — glorification as limit]
The integral term is the mathematical mechanism by which God's work in the believer drives residual sin to zero over the course of sanctification. It operates slowly but eliminates what K_p and K_d cannot.
And I am sure of this, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ. Philippians 1:6 (ESV) — the integral converges; the work completes

Separator Tests & Falsification

What passes, what would destroy this mapping
Swap Test — Passed
Replace "PID controller" with "providence" and "plant" with "human agency." The feedback structure holds exactly. The controller-plant distinction maps onto the sovereignty-free will distinction without remainder. The three PID terms (proportional / conscience, integral / sanctification, derivative / prophetic warning) are non-interchangeable in both domains — swapping them produces engineering nonsense in Domain A and theological nonsense in Domain B.
Bidirectional Prediction — Passed
Forward (control → theology): Removing K_i should leave steady-state error → theology confirms that without ongoing sanctification, residual sin persists indefinitely (simul justus et peccator never resolves). Reverse (theology → control): God's intervention should have timing constraints (delayed response degrades stability) → phase margin precisely models this. "He who is coming will come and will not delay" (Heb 10:37) is a stability requirement.
Non-Interchangeability — Passed
Conscience (K_p) is not sanctification (K_i). Sanctification is not prophetic warning (K_d). The three are structurally distinct — each responds to a different function of the error signal (instantaneous value, integral, derivative). Collapsing them would be like claiming conscience alone can eliminate remaining sin (K_p eliminates steady-state error) — which the isomorphism specifically predicts is impossible.
 Kill Condition — Falsification

Primary: If providential action showed no feedback characteristics — if God's governance were purely open-loop (no response to human state, just predetermined outputs regardless of error) — the mapping fails. The isomorphism requires a feedback God: one whose governance is shaped by the actual measured error, not just an a priori plan. Scripture describes a God who responds, relents, is moved, and adjusts — a feedback system, not a pre-recorded sequence.

Secondary: If God's governance replaced the plant entirely — if divine sovereignty meant human agency was eliminated (hard determinism) — the controller/plant distinction would collapse. The plant G_human(s) must retain its own transfer function for the architecture to hold. Any theological position that makes human responses merely epiphenomenal (not genuine outputs of a real plant) falsifies the mapping.

Tertiary: If conscience, sanctification, and prophetic warning showed identical behavioral signatures — if they could not be empirically distinguished in their mode of action — the three-term structure would be unfalsifiable. But empirically they are distinct: conscience is immediate, sanctification is slow and accumulative, and prophetic warning is anticipatory.
