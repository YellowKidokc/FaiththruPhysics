# Control Theory :: Providence

## DOMAINS

**Domain A — Control Theory**
The PID controller is the workhorse of engineering feedback systems: C(s) = K_p + K_i/s + K_d*s. The proportional term (K_p) responds to present error. The integral term (K_i) accumulates past error to eliminate steady-state offset. The derivative term (K_d) anticipates future error by responding to the rate of change. The controller output drives the plant (the system being controlled), but does not replace it — the plant retains its own transfer function G(s). Stability requires proper gain and phase margins.

**Domain B — Theology**
Providence is God's ongoing governance of creation. It operates through conscience (present conviction of error), sanctification (accumulated transformation over time), and prophetic warning (anticipatory correction). The critical theological claim: providence does NOT override human agency. God steers but does not replace the human will — the "plant" G_human(s) retains its own dynamics. This resolves the free will / sovereignty tension: the controller is not the plant.

| 12 | Phase margin | Timing of divine intervention | How much delay the system can tolerate before losing stability |
| 13 | Adaptive control | Progressive revelation | Controller parameters change as the system (humanity) changes |

**Key Insight — Free Will Resolution**: The controller does NOT replace the plant. G_human(s) retains its own transfer function — its own dynamics, its own response characteristics. God's providential control operates on the input to the plant, not by substituting for the plant. This is the engineering-precise version of "God steers but doesn't override."

## TESTS

**Swap Test**: PASSED. Replace "PID controller" with "providence" and "plant" with "human agency" — the feedback structure holds exactly. The controller-plant distinction maps onto the sovereignty-free will distinction without remainder. The three PID terms map onto three theologically distinct modes of divine action.

**Bidirectional Prediction**: Control theory predicts that removing K_i should leave steady-state error → theology confirms that without sanctification (long-term correction), residual sin persists. Theology predicts that God's intervention should have timing constraints → phase margin precisely models this.

**Falsification Condition**: If providential action showed no feedback characteristics — i.e., if God's governance were purely open-loop (no response to human state) or purely deterministic (replacing the plant entirely) — the mapping would collapse. Scripture describes a feedback God who responds to human action while preserving human agency.
