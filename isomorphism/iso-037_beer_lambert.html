Domain A — Optics (Beer-Lambert)
Attenuation of Light Through an Absorbing Medium
The Beer-Lambert Law (Beer 1852, Lambert 1760) describes how light attenuates as it passes through an absorbing medium. It is one of the most precisely validated laws in all of analytical chemistry — used to measure blood glucose, atmospheric CO₂, industrial pollutants, and astronomical redshift. Its precision is not approximate; it is exact for monochromatic light in a homogeneous medium.
Beer-Lambert Law — Master Equation

Transparent vs. Opaque Media

Transparent medium (α ≈ 0): Signal passes with minimal loss — clear glass, pure water. Opaque medium (large α): Signal rapidly extinguished — ink, heavy metal solutions. A few millimeters absorbs most of the light. The character of the medium is entirely encoded in α. The light has no say in the matter.
