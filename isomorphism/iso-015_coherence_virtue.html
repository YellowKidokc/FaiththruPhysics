The Claim

What is being asserted and how it could be falsified
Process Isomorphism · Information-Theoretic · Level 2 (Structural Analogy) · Registry Entry

Virtue is that which increases coherence (ΔC > 0). Sin is that which decreases it (ΔC < 0). Conscience functions as a local coherence gradient detector — it senses whether an action moves the system toward or away from coherence, without requiring explicit calculation.

This claim maps information-theoretic coherence C[χ] onto the natural law tradition's account of virtue and vice. Just as coherence in information systems is measurable and directional, moral consequences are observable over time. Just as conscience senses the local gradient of coherence, it functions as a moral instrument detecting alignment with the underlying order of reality.

Honest classification: all correspondences are currently at Level 2 (Structural Analogy). The mapping has suggestive empirical support but lacks bidirectional operation-preserving maps and quantitative predictions sufficient for Level 3 promotion.

Scope Limitation — Level 2 Honest

This isomorphism is currently a structural analogy, not a proven isomorphism. The direction virtue → ΔC > 0 is plausible and has empirical support. The reverse — can we predict virtue from coherence metrics? — is not yet demonstrated. Confidence is medium-low. Treat this entry as a productive research hypothesis, not a confirmed structural correspondence.

02

Domain A — Information Theory

Coherence C[χ] and Shannon entropy as the physics side
Information theory provides a rigorous, measurable framework for coherence and disorder. The Shannon entropy measures disorder; coherence moves inversely. The key quantity for this isomorphism is ΔC — the directional change in coherence produced by an action or process.
Shannon Entropy — Measure of Disorder
H = -∑ pᵢ log pᵢ
Shannon (1948). H is maximized when all outcomes are equally probable (maximum disorder) and minimized when one outcome is certain (maximum order). Coherence C[χ] moves inversely to entropy in structured systems: higher coherence = lower informational entropy within the relevant degrees of freedom.
Coherence Change — The Key Quantity
ΔC = C[χ]₁ - C[χ]₀ ΔC > 0 → System becomes more coherent ΔC < 0 → System becomes less coherent ΔC = 0 → No net change in coherence
ΔC is evaluated for the action or process in question, integrated over the appropriate time horizon and scope (see Swap Test for why scope matters).

Coherence is not merely aesthetic order — it is a measurable property of information systems. A coherent system's internal states are mutually consistent; contradictions, noise, and disorder degrade coherence. The coherence gradient ∇C tells you which direction increases coherence at any point in the system's state space.

03

Domain B — Natural Law Theology

Virtue as alignment with moral structure; conscience as gradient sensor
They show that the work of the law is written on their hearts, while their conscience also bears witness, and their conflicting thoughts accuse or even excuse them. Romans 2:14-15

The natural law tradition (Aquinas, Aristotle, Paul) holds that moral truths are embedded in the structure of reality itself — not imposed externally but constitutive of what human beings and communities are. Virtue is alignment with that structure. Vice is deviation from it. The consequences of vice are real and observable over time, not merely conventional.

Conscience functions on this model as an internal moral sensor — not a social construct but a genuine faculty for detecting whether an action coheres with the moral order. Conscience does not calculate; it detects. This is the functional analog of the coherence gradient: conscience senses ∇C locally, issuing a reading that an action is virtue-directed (ΔC > 0) or vice-directed (ΔC < 0).

The Key Correspondence

The natural law tradition's claim that conscience is a faculty (not merely a social feeling) maps directly onto the information-theoretic claim that a coherence gradient is a measurable quantity. Both claims assert that there is an objective signal — structural, not merely conventional — that virtuous action tracks.

The Key Claim

Virtue is that which increases coherence. Sin is that which decreases it. Conscience functions as a local coherence gradient detector. If the natural law tradition is correct that moral truths are structural (not conventional), then measuring coherence change is measuring something real about virtue and vice.

05

Evidence

Empirical support — honest about what it does and does not show
World Values Survey — Trust as Coherence Proxy

Societies with higher interpersonal trust (a plausible proxy for social coherence) consistently show better outcomes across health, economic, and institutional metrics. Trust erosion — social decoherence — correlates with institutional decay, increased crime, reduced economic activity, and deteriorating public health. The World Values Survey documents this across 90+ countries over four decades.

Honest Caveat

This evidence is suggestive, not conclusive. Trust is a proxy for coherence, not a direct measure. The correlation between trust-as-coherence and flourishing is real but the causal mechanism is not established. Alternative explanations (institutions, wealth, geography) are not ruled out. This evidence supports the isomorphism as a research direction — it does not confirm it.

For this isomorphism to reach Level 3, we would need: (1) a direct information-theoretic measure of coherence applied to human communities, (2) a demonstrated correlation between that measure and independent moral assessments, and (3) bidirectional predictive power — not just "virtue → coherence" but "coherence → virtue (or conditions for virtue)."

06

Separator Tests

Swap test, strain points, and what it would take to falsify
Swap Test — PARTIAL
The mapping works in the primary direction: virtue → ΔC > 0, vice → ΔC < 0. However, sin can temporarily increase local coherence. A well-organized criminal syndicate has high internal coherence — it runs efficiently, commands loyalty, achieves its objectives. The mapping requires specifying scope: sin decreases global or long-term coherence even when it increases local or short-term coherence. This is a genuine strain point, not a fatal flaw, but it prevents a clean PASSED verdict.
Scope Constraint Required
The coherence metric must be evaluated at the appropriate scale: the global system (including all affected parties over the full time horizon), not local subsystems over short periods. This is not ad hoc — the natural law tradition has always insisted on the common good evaluated in its fullness. The constraint is internal to the mapping, not imported to save it.
Conscience as Gradient Detector — Supported
The claim that conscience detects local coherence gradient (rather than calculating global consequences) explains both its strengths and its limitations. Conscience is fast and local — it gives immediate directional readings. It fails when local and global gradients diverge (as in the criminal syndicate case). This matches empirical psychology: conscience is an affective signal, not a deliberative faculty. The isomorphism explains the architecture of conscience without requiring full consequentialist calculation.
 Falsification Condition

If moral improvement showed no correlation with measurable coherence metrics at any scale — that is, if virtuous societies were no more coherent (by any information-theoretic measure) than vicious ones — the mapping breaks. Specifically: if high-trust, low-corruption societies (virtue proxies) showed the same entropy distribution as low-trust, high-corruption societies (vice proxies), the coherence-virtue correspondence fails.

A stronger falsification: if a community pursuing virtue consistently became less coherent by information-theoretic measures, while a community pursuing vice became more coherent — with no scope adjustment possible — the mapping inverts and must be abandoned.
