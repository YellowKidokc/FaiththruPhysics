# ISO-026: Addiction ↔ Sin — Tolerance IS Entropy in the Reward System

## DOMAINS

**Domain A — Neuroscience / Pharmacology**
Addiction follows a deterministic trajectory: initial reward → tolerance (dR/dt < 0, receptor sensitivity decreasing) → escalation → dependence → withdrawal → relapse vulnerability. The allostatic model (Koob & Le Moal) describes a progressive shift in hedonic set-point. Key features: (1) tolerance is irreversible in degree, (2) cross-tolerance generalizes vulnerability, (3) external intervention is statistically necessary for sustained recovery, (4) neuroplasticity enables but does not guarantee rewiring.

**Domain B — Theology (Hamartiology / Romans 7)**
Sin follows a parallel trajectory: initial pleasure → diminishing returns → escalation → bondage → conviction/emptiness → relapse. Paul's description in Romans 7:15-20 ("I do not do the good I want to do, but the evil I do not want to do — this I keep on doing") is a phenomenological description of addictive compulsion. AA's Steps 1-2 map precisely: Step 1 (powerlessness) = Terminus Sui; Step 2 (higher power) = grace coupling.

## TESTS

**Swap Test**: PASSED. The substitutions generate substantive, testable claims:
- "Tolerance IS entropy in the reward system" — this is not metaphor. Receptor downregulation is a literal increase in disorder (decreased signal-to-noise ratio in reward circuitry). The Second Law applies to neural systems.
- "Original sin is genetic vulnerability" — predicts that sin propensity has heritable components, which is consistent with behavioral genetics findings on impulsivity, aggression, and addiction risk.
- "Sanctification is neuroplasticity" — predicts that spiritual growth should show measurable neural correlates (changes in prefrontal-limbic connectivity), which is consistent with meditation/contemplative prayer neuroimaging studies.

**Bidirectional Prediction**:
- Forward (neuroscience → theology): Addiction science predicts that willpower-only approaches to sin will fail at population scale. This matches the theological claim that "the law" (moral effort) cannot save.
- Reverse (theology → neuroscience): The theological insistence on community (ecclesiology) predicts that isolated recovery attempts will have lower success rates. This is confirmed by addiction research (social support is the strongest predictor of sustained recovery).

**Falsification**: If addiction recovery were achievable purely through willpower with zero external support at statistically significant rates. This would break the Terminus Sui → grace coupling mapping. Current evidence strongly supports the mapping: isolated willpower-based recovery rates are extremely low.
