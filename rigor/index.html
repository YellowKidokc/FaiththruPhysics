Truth-Finding Protocol

A universal framework for adversarial truth-resolution. A systematic, evidence-based methodology for resolving polarized disputes between opposing factions using the scientific method.

When two sides disagree and neither will yield, the question isn't who argues louder. The question is: what does the evidence actually say, tested against every way it could be wrong?

The Problem

Most disputes never reach resolution because both sides argue from conclusion backward. Evidence is cherry-picked. Context is stripped. The loudest voice wins, not the truest one.

OpenIntel inverts this. Start from the evidence. Classify it by reliability tier. Test every way the evidence could be fabricated, forged, or mistaken. Compute the probability that the entire evidence base is wrong. Then — and only then — render a verdict.

Evidence Tier System

Not all evidence is equal. The tier system weights each piece by its reliability and resistance to fabrication.

T1

Physical / Forensic Evidence

Artifacts, manuscripts, archaeological finds, physical traces. Hardest to fabricate. Highest weight.

T2

Documentary / Textual Evidence

Contemporary written records, official documents, independent attestations.

T3

Corroborative / Circumstantial Evidence

Consistent patterns, supporting context, logical inference from established facts.

T4

Testimonial / Expert Evidence

Expert analysis, qualified testimony, scholarly consensus.

T5

Hearsay / Tradition

Oral tradition, secondhand accounts, unverified claims. Lowest weight but still catalogued.

The Fakery Matrix

Every piece of evidence is tested against 10 independent forgery constraints. Each constraint asks: could this have been fabricated? The compound probability of ALL constraints being satisfied by forgery is the real measure.

F1–F5 Material Constraints

F1 Material Access — Did the forger have physical access to the required materials?

F2 Technical Skill — Did the required fabrication technology exist?

F3 Time Window — Was there enough time to execute the forgery?

F4 Distribution — Could forged evidence be distributed without detection?

F5 Consistency — Does the forgery maintain internal consistency?

F6–F10 Social Constraints

F6 Motive Alignment — Did the forger benefit from the forgery?

F7 Hostile Witness — Did enemies confirm the evidence?

F8 Behavioral Cost — Did believers pay real costs for their belief?

F9 Independent Attestation — Do multiple independent sources confirm?

F10 Predictive Power — Does the evidence predict things the forger couldn't know?

The Compound Rule

If any single constraint is rated IMPOSSIBLE, the forgery hypothesis fails at that constraint. If multiple constraints are rated IMPOSSIBLE or EXTREME, the compound probability of successful forgery drops to effectively zero. The math doesn't care about the narrative. It asks one question: could every piece of evidence have been fabricated, simultaneously, by the same conspiracy? Run the numbers.
