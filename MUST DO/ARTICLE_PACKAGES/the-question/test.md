# The Question — Test Path

## Claims Made

1. **Both identity claims exceed empirical observation.** Trans identity claims internal gender despite external biology. Christian identity claims spiritual rebirth despite no physical change.

2. **The definition controls the outcome.** Whoever controls the definition of "woman" or "truth" wins the debate before arguments begin.

3. **The DSM applies inconsistent diagnostic criteria.** Persistent belief contrary to evidence is treated as delusion in one case and affirmed in another, with no principled distinction beyond duration/sincerity.

4. **Brain evidence is underdetermined.** ENIGMA (2021) shows a unique trans neurological phenotype, not a "female brain in a male body." Neuroplasticity research shows all deeply held beliefs reshape brain structure.

5. **The harm vector is inverted between the two claims.** Trans: "your refusal harms me." Christian: "your rejection harms you."

6. **Historical anchoring differs.** Christianity was operational before definitional (1st century practice, named in Acts 11:26). Trans identity went definitional before operational (de Beauvoir 1949 academic theory → later clinical implementation).

## Strongest Objections (Steelmanned)

- **Against the comparison itself:** These are categorically different claims — one is about gender, one is about metaphysics. Comparing them is a category error.
  - *Response:* The comparison is structural, not moral. Both make invisible identity claims requiring external recognition. The structure is identical even if the domains differ.

- **Against the DSM critique:** Gender dysphoria and grandiose delusion involve different phenomenology and clinical presentations.
  - *Response:* The diagnostic STRUCTURE is the same (persistent conviction contrary to evidence, resistant to contradictory evidence). The distinction in treatment must be principled, not just asserted.

- **Against the brain evidence critique:** Adolescent studies show sex-atypical brain patterns before any social transition.
  - *Response:* Pre-transition adolescent studies are the strongest counter, but still cannot rule out early experiential neuroplasticity. Cross-sectional designs cannot establish causation.

## Kill Conditions

- Find a stable society that functions without shared definitions
- Find a case where redefining a foundational category had no downstream cultural effects
- Show that the DSM treats persistent belief contrary to evidence consistently across all diagnoses
- Produce a longitudinal (not cross-sectional) brain study that rules out neuroplasticity
