# The Question — Plain Path

This paper examines a structural symmetry between two identity claims: transgender identity and Christian faith.

Both make claims that go beyond what can be directly observed. A trans woman says she is a woman despite male biology. A Christian says he is born again despite no visible change. Both ask society to accept the claim. Both say refusal causes harm.

The paper compares these claims across four dimensions: what each claims about reality (ontology), where each gets its authority, what each asks others to do (recognition), and what each says about the body (embodiment).

## Key findings

The claims share the same logical structure but differ in three critical ways.

First, the harm vector is inverted. The trans claim says your refusal harms me. The Christian claim says your rejection harms you, and the Christian response is pursuit, not punishment.

Second, the historical anchor differs. Christianity operated for centuries before it was named (Acts 11:26). The modern concept of gender as separate from sex was defined academically in 1949 (Simone de Beauvoir) before it became operational.

Third, the authority source differs. The Christian claim points to an external author (Scripture, Christ). The trans claim is internally sourced — the individual is the final authority on their own identity.

## The DSM question

The DSM-5 classifies persistent belief in being a king as grandiose delusion requiring treatment, but persistent belief in being the opposite sex as gender dysphoria requiring affirmation. Same diagnostic structure — different treatment protocols. The only stated distinction is duration and sincerity.

## Brain evidence

The ENIGMA consortium study (Mueller et al. 2021, N=803) does not show that trans women have female brains. It shows a unique neurological phenotype matching neither sex. The neuroplasticity literature shows that deeply held beliefs reshape brain structure across domains (London taxi drivers, meditators, political ideologues). The same brain data supports both "innate" and "adapted to belief" interpretations. The evidence is underdetermined.

## What the paper does not do

It does not declare a winner. It presents both cases at their strongest and lets the structural comparison speak for itself. The companion papers (The Floor, Two Worlds) take the analysis further.
