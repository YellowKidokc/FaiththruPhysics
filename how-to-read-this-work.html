How to Read This Work

This page applies to every series on this site. It explains what is being attempted, how claims are made, and where the known limits are. Read this once. It covers everything.

What This Work Is

  • A structured attempt to identify recurring patterns across independent domains — physics, information theory, and theology.
  • A framework for thinking about collapse, entropy, coherence, and information beyond any single field.
  • A set of constrained models that suggest possible mechanisms, not completed proofs.
  • An invitation to examine whether multiple systems may share underlying structure.

What This Work Is Not

  • Not a claim that physics and theology are proven identical systems.
  • Not a replacement for established physics or formal mathematical models.
  • Not dependent on any single interpretation being accepted.
  • Not a demand for agreement — only for consideration.

How Claims Are Made

  • Patterns are identified when multiple defining properties align across domains.
  • Structures are proposed when relationships between variables can be formally mapped.
  • Mechanisms are suggested when sequences, constraints, and transitions can be described.
  • Full equivalence is never claimed unless independently demonstrated.

Known Limits

  • Many variables are not yet formally defined or independently measurable.
  • Some mappings rely on structural interpretation rather than direct physical derivation.
  • Not all alternative explanations have been ruled out.
  • The framework is incomplete and actively developing.
  • Every article includes its own audit section identifying specific gaps and overreach.

Reader Expectation

This work is best approached as a structured exploration of possibility. The author is not asking you to believe. He is asking you to evaluate the structure. If the patterns are real, they will withstand scrutiny and refinement. If they are not, they will fail under the same process. Either outcome is acceptable.

Where our model limits what God can be, the limitation is ours, not His. We offer this as worship, not containment.
