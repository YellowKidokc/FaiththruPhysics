# The Three Truths
### Before we argue about anything, we need to agree on three things.

---

Not Christian things. Not atheist things. Not religious things at all.

Three things that every human being on the planet already accepts the moment they open their mouth to disagree with someone. Three things you cannot deny without using. Three things that 70,000 people across 42 countries — Muslim, Hindu, Christian, Buddhist, atheist, agnostic — already demonstrate without being asked.

This isn't theology. This is the floor.

---

## Truth One: Truth Exists

Before we debate God, the Bible, science, politics, or what you had for breakfast — do you believe truth exists? Is there such a thing as something being actually true or actually false?

If you say yes, we have shared ground. Everything that follows — every claim, every argument, every piece of evidence — is subject to being true or false. Neither of us gets to retreat into "well, that's just your truth" when the conversation gets uncomfortable. Truth exists. We agreed. That door is closed.

If you say no — if you say "there is no truth" or "truth is relative" or "there's no absolute truth" — then you have a problem. Because you just made a truth claim. You told me something you believe is actually the case. You used the thing you're denying to deny it.

This isn't a trick. This is a structural feature of reality. You cannot deny truth without asserting truth. Every attempt to rephrase it — "well, what I mean is..." — is another truth claim. The faculty you're using to evaluate my statement is the same faculty that proves truth exists.

Aristotle identified this 2,400 years ago. The principle of non-contradiction is the one thing you cannot deny without standing on it. It's not a belief. It's not a philosophy. It's the floor.

So: truth exists. We agree. Let's move on.

---

## Truth Two: You Can Know Things Beyond a Lab

Science is extraordinary. It cured polio. It put people on the moon. It sequenced the human genome. Nobody here is against science.

But is science the *only* way to know what's true?

If you say no — if you agree that logic, mathematics, philosophy, history, and personal experience can also produce knowledge — then we have more shared ground. You can't dismiss the Bible, theology, or philosophy just because "it's not science." You have to engage with claims on their own terms. Mathematical proofs aren't experiments. Historical testimony isn't a lab result. Logical deductions aren't peer-reviewed. But they produce knowledge. You know 2+2=4 and you didn't need a control group to get there.

If you say yes — if you say "science is the only way to know truth" — then I have one question: Is that claim itself a scientific finding? Did you run an experiment to determine that? Was there a control group? Was it published in a journal?

No. You just used philosophy to tell me only science produces knowledge. The statement "all knowledge is scientific knowledge" is a philosophical claim, not a scientific one. It fails its own test.

This is called scientism. Even most scientists reject it. Physics can't tell you whether murder is wrong. Mathematics isn't an empirical science. Logic precedes science — you need logic to DO science, so logic can't be a product of science. A hammer is great for nails. It's terrible for screws. Science is a tool for finding truth. It is not the only tool.

So: truth can be known in multiple ways. We agree. Let's move on.

---

## Truth Three: Right and Wrong Are Real

This is where it matters.

Are there things that are actually right or actually wrong? Not opinions. Not preferences. Not "what works for me." Actual moral facts — things that are wrong regardless of whether anyone agrees.

If you say yes, then we agree on the most important thing: there IS a right answer about slavery, about murder, about oppression, about how we treat each other. We can discover what it is. We can be wrong about it. But the answer exists, and it exists independently of our feelings about it.

If you say no — if you say morality is "just subjective" or "just cultural" — then I need to ask: Is it WRONG to say that? Because the moment you say "there are no moral facts," you've made a moral claim. You're telling me how things OUGHT to be understood. And if there's no actual right or wrong, then you can't say secular humanism is BETTER than Christianity. Better requires a standard. No moral facts, no standard, no argument.

Every debate about society — every claim that one system produces "better outcomes" than another — requires moral facts to exist. Without them, "better" is just your preference, and the strong impose their preferences on the weak. That's not progress. That's power without principle.

But here's the thing: you already know moral facts are real. The data proves it.

In 2020, researchers published the largest cross-cultural study of moral judgment ever conducted. 70,000 people. 42 countries. 10 languages. Muslim countries, Hindu countries, Christian countries, secular countries, countries in Asia, Africa, Europe, and the Americas. The finding: there IS a universal moral pattern. Cultures vary on how strongly they hold certain preferences. But the direction — the basic structure of what's right and what's wrong — is the same everywhere.

In 2025 and 2026, Pew Research surveyed 25 countries and found that across every single one, at least half the population agreed that betrayal is morally wrong. Not a slim majority. A median of 77%.

A researcher studying this data put it simply: people across all cultural contexts are moral realists. They believe moral claims about what's right and wrong are objectively true. What varies is how those beliefs apply in specific situations — not whether they exist.

Nobody taught 70,000 people across 42 countries to agree. Nobody coordinated this. The moral pattern is already there. It's built in.

The question is: built in by what?

---

## The Thing Behind All Three

Every truth claim you evaluated while reading this — every moment you thought "that's right" or "that's wrong" or "I'm not sure" — you were using something. A capacity. A faculty. The ability to assess, weigh, judge.

Animals don't do this. Animals react to stimuli. They don't evaluate whether their reaction is correct. A wolf doesn't ask whether killing the rabbit is moral. A dolphin doesn't wonder if it ought to help another dolphin. They just do what they do.

You don't. Every input passes through a layer of evaluation before you act. Should I? Is this right? What will happen if I do? You can't turn it off. You can suppress it. You can override it. But you can't un-know the difference between good and evil. It's irreversible.

This is what separates you from every other creature on the planet. Not language — parrots talk. Not tool use — crows use tools. Not social structure — ants have that. The thing that makes you human is that you EVALUATE. You judge. You stand between stimulus and response and you CHOOSE based on an assessment of right and wrong, true and false, better and worse.

To deny this faculty is special — to say "the judgment layer isn't important" or "it's just neurons firing" — is itself a judgment. You used the thing to deny the thing. Self-refuting on contact.

And here's what's interesting: the oldest text in the world that people argue about identifies exactly when this capacity turned on. Genesis 3. Before the tree: no moral evaluation. They were naked and it didn't register. After the tree: moral evaluation online, irreversible. "Your eyes will be opened, and you will know good and evil." God confirms it in verse 22: "The man has become like one of us, knowing good and evil."

One text. One event. One transition. Before: animal-regime. After: moral-regime. Binary. Irreversible.

70,000 people across 42 countries are living proof that it's still on.

---

## What This Means

These three truths aren't arguments for God. They're the prerequisites for ANY argument about anything.

You can't debate whether Christianity is true unless truth exists. You can't evaluate the evidence unless knowledge extends beyond lab experiments. You can't say one system is better than another unless moral facts are real. And you can't do any of these things without the judgment layer — the one thing every human has, every worldview depends on, and only one text in human history explains the origin of.

If you accept all three — and you do, because you've been using all three since you started reading — then the ground is set. Now we can talk about what's true, how to find it, and what it means.

Everything else on this site builds from here.
