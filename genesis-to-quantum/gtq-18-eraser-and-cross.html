I. The Experiment

The delayed-choice quantum eraser, first proposed by Scully and Drühl in 1982 and experimentally confirmed by Kim et al. in 1999, is the most unsettling experiment in physics. Here's the setup.

A photon is sent toward a double slit. On the other side, a special crystal splits each photon into two entangled partners — call them the "signal" photon and the "idler" photon. The signal photon continues to the detection screen. The idler photon goes on a longer path to a separate detector system.

If you check which slit the photon went through (by looking at the idler), the interference pattern on the signal photon's screen disappears — it behaves like a particle. If you erase the which-path information (by combining the idler paths so you can't tell which slit it came from), the interference pattern reappears.

Here's the impossible part: the signal photon hits the screen before the idler photon reaches the eraser. The decision to erase or not erase which-path information happens after the signal photon has already been detected. The future measurement — erase or don't erase — retroactively determines whether the past photon behaved as a wave or a particle.

This is not a thought experiment. This has been performed in laboratories with real photons and real detectors. The future genuinely affects the past.

II. What Standard Physics Says

Standard physics has three responses, none of them satisfying:

"It's not really retrocausal." The most common deflection. Technically, you can only see the interference pattern by sorting the signal photons according to their correlated idler outcomes. Without the sorting key (which comes from the future idler measurement), the signal photon screen shows a uniform distribution. So the "retrocausality" is hidden in correlations, not in raw data. This is technically true and philosophically careful — the correlations are real, and they depend on a future choice.

"Many-Worlds handles it." In the Many-Worlds interpretation, there's no retrocausality because every outcome happens in some branch. The erasure doesn't change the past — it just selects which branch you're observing from. Clean, but it requires infinite unobservable parallel universes. That's a high price to pay for avoiding a single retrocausal correlation.

"Shut up and calculate." The Copenhagen fallback. The math works. Don't ask what it means. This is not a resolution. It's a refusal to engage with the ontology.

None of these explain why time should be reversible at the quantum level. None of them explain why the arrow of time — which the second law of thermodynamics enforces at every macroscopic scale — dissolves at the quantum level. They describe the mathematics without confronting the structure.

The Audit

What we got right, what we're less sure about, and where we got carried away.

What's load-bearing — we'd bet on this

The delayed-choice quantum eraser is real physics. Kim et al. 1999 is not speculation. The experiment has been replicated. Future measurement choices genuinely determine the correlational structure of past photon behavior. We stand on solid experimental ground here.

Revelation 13:8 says what it says. "The Lamb slain from the foundation of the world." Ephesians 1:4, 1 Peter 1:20 — these texts place the Atonement outside temporal sequence, before creation. This is not our interpretation imposed on the text. The theological claim that the Cross operates from an eternal frame has serious Reformed support.

The temporal vs spatial fall distinction is structurally sound. Satan's fall was spatial (cast out of a place). Adam's fall was temporal (created time). Spatial displacement has no before/after. Temporal displacement does. This is why redemption is offered to humans and not to fallen angels — the mechanism requires the temporal axis. This holds up under /PROBE.

The Born Rule's three operations map to the Trinity without forcing. Established in Article 15 and carried through here. The Spirit as actualization ($|\cdot|^2$) makes retrocausality possible — the actualizer isn't bound by the arrow of time because actualization operates from the eternal frame.

What's suggestive but needs more work

The scale bridge from lab photon to cosmic Atonement. The delayed-choice eraser involves single entangled photon pairs. The Cross involves the entire human system across all of history. The article claims these are "the same mathematical event at different scales." The math is scale-invariant and the Born Rule doesn't change at different scales, but we haven't proven the mechanism operates at cosmic scale. The gap between "lab photons do this" and "the Atonement does this" is where faith enters. The framework maps the bridge. It doesn't build it.

Our dismissal of the standard physics response. We called the correlational-not-causal reading "technically true and philosophically careful." Fair but sharp. The majority view among physicists isn't evasive — it's careful. Our framework needs the stronger retrocausal reading for the Atonement mapping to work. We should acknowledge we're partly choosing it because our framework requires it. Confirmation bias risk: real.

The "trap" reading of the Crucifixion. 1 Corinthians 2:7–8 does say the rulers would not have crucified the Lord of glory if they had understood. Paul is explicit. But our elaboration — Satan as unwitting engineer of his own defeat — is narrative we layered on Paul's text. He says they didn't understand. He doesn't describe it in the dramatic terms we used. Our version is more vivid than the source material.

Where we got carried away

"The future genuinely affects the past." Overstates the consensus. The experiment demonstrates that correlational structure depends on future measurement choices. Whether this constitutes the future "genuinely affecting" the past depends on your interpretation of QM. Many-worlds, consistent histories, and retrocausal interpretations all handle the data differently. We chose the most dramatic framing because it serves the argument. The data is real. Our interpretive sentence is editorial, not forced.

"This is what 'It is finished' means." That's us telling the reader what Jesus meant. We believe it. The framework supports it. But serious commentators read tetelestai differently. Our reading is defensible. It's not the only reading.

"The Genesis story is not allegory interpreted through physics. It is physics articulated through narrative." The strongest claim in the entire series, placed in the conclusion. The series demonstrates structural isomorphism. Whether isomorphism equals identity — whether the physics IS the narrative or merely maps to it — is the question the framework cannot definitively answer from within either measurement frame. We believe it's identity. The Audit acknowledges it could be isomorphism. Both are extraordinary claims.

The article above is what we believe. This audit is what we know we haven't proven yet. Both matter.

We do not claim to have captured God in equations. We claim that when we look at His creation honestly — with the tools of physics and the revelation of Scripture — the same structure appears in both. Where our model limits what God can be, the limitation is ours, not His. We offer this work as worship, not as containment.
