Phase I — State + Entropy + Time
1
Collapse (Irreversibility)

Start here. Before time, before entropy, before structure — there is a system in superposition. Something selects a basis. The state resolves. Everything after follows from this.

State$Y \sim p(y)$  superposed / unresolved
Operation$M_b$ : measurement in basis $b$
Update$Y \to y_i$ with $p_i$
Entropy$S_{\text{post}} < S_{\text{pre}}$  conditional entropy reduces
PathNon-invertible  information loss to environment
ResultSingle event ⇒ global state update (non-reversible)
2
Superposition (Unresolved Domain)

If collapse produces irreversibility, what existed before collapse? A domain where no basis has been selected. Maximum entropy under constraints. All outcomes latent. This is the pre-measurement state — and it requires no observer to exist.

Pre-measure$H(Y)$ maximal under constraints
ConstraintNo $M_b$ ⇒ no selection
Structure$\forall b$, outcomes latent
Interpret"No decision" ≡ no basis selection
3
Dual Frames (Consistency)

Sections 1 and 2 give us two domains — one where all states coexist, one where they resolve sequentially. These are not contradictions. They are different projections of the same joint distribution, valid under their own algebras. Both true. Neither complete alone.

Frames$F_{\Sigma_F}$ : global (all states co-present)  ·  $F_{\Sigma_D}$ : local (sequential)
Mapping$\pi : \Sigma_F \to \Sigma_D$
Consistency$\text{Truth}_F \neq \text{Truth}_D$  both valid under respective σ-algebras
ResolutionDifferent projections of same joint distribution  no contradiction
4
Time (Arrow)

Collapse (Section 1) produces irreversibility. Irreversibility produces ordering. Ordering is time. Time does not exist in the fundamental space — it emerges when measurement forces transitions into a sequence. The arrow of time is the entropy gradient created by collapse.

Define$\Delta S = S(t + \Delta t) - S(t)$
Arrow$\Delta S \geq 0$ in $\Sigma_D$
EventTransition induces ordering: $t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_n$
InterpretTime ≡ ordered information increase  entropy gradient
Phase II — Structure + Limits
5
Substrate (Broken Phase)

If collapse created time and irreversibility (Sections 1, 4), then the derivative space $\Sigma_D$ is not the original reality — it is the broken-symmetry phase. The laws we measure are effective laws, valid only post-break. The coupling between spaces was severed at the transition.

Model$\Sigma_D$ = broken-symmetry phase of $\Sigma_F$
Order param$\varphi \neq 0$ after break
Coupling$C_{\text{orig}} \to C_0$  severance
ObservationLaws in $\Sigma_D$ = effective  post-break
6
Triadic Constraint (Minimal Closure)

The substrate requires a generator, a state, and a relation between them. Two components cannot close over transformations — the system leaks. Three is the minimum for self-consistent evolution. This is a structural claim about closure, not a theological assertion.

RequireGenerator $(G)$, State $(S)$, Relation $(R)$
Binary fails$\{G, S\}$ lacks closure over transformations
Triad$T = (G, S, R)$  ⇒  $\text{Closure}(T) \to \text{stable dynamics}$
ClaimMinimal structure for self-consistent evolution $\geq 3$
7
Measurement Limits (Observer)

Collapse requires a measurement operator (Section 1), but the operator is not consciousness. The observer selects the basis — the system selects the outcome. Information extracted is bounded by the interaction, not by the observer's awareness. This constrains all claims about "consciousness causes collapse."

Measurementoutcome $\sim p(y \mid b, \text{interaction})$
Not requiredhuman consciousness $\notin$ necessary set
Observer roleselects $b$, not $y_i$
Information$I(\text{outcome}; \text{system})$ constrained by interaction, not intent
Phase III — Interpretation + Regimes
8
Delayed Choice (Information Ordering)

If the fundamental frame (Section 3) holds all states co-present, then a choice made "later" in the derivative frame can condition statistics "earlier" — without violating causality. This is not backward causation. It is an update of the joint distribution across frames. The dual-frame structure predicted this.

ExperimentChoice at $t_2$ conditions statistics at $t_1$
Formal$p(y \mid \text{choice}_{t_2}) \neq p(y)$ marginally
ConstraintNo signal to past  no causal violation
InterpretUpdate of joint distribution, not backward causation
9
Regimes (Same System, Different Constraints)

The coupling architecture $C$ is not fixed. Different values of $C$ produce different observable behavior from the same underlying system. This explains why the same physics appears to operate differently across regimes — the substrates didn't change, the coupling did. One system. Multiple expressions.

States$C_0$ : sparse, mediated, fragile  ·  $C_1$ : distributed, direct, stable
DynamicsSame $\Sigma_F$, $\Sigma_D$  —  different $C$ ⇒ different observables
ConclusionOne system, regime-dependent behavior
Phase IV — Mechanism
10
Coupling-Architecture Modification

Sections 1–9 describe the architecture. This section describes the repair. If the substrate broke through symmetry-breaking (Section 5), then restoration requires the reverse sequence — localization into the broken phase, coherent stabilization under its constraints, release of accumulated energy, and irreversible update of the coupling constant. Five steps. Each one necessary. Skip any step and the architecture remains broken.

Step 1Localization:  $\Psi_F \to \psi_D$  fundamental state enters derivative space (SSB)
WhyThe fix must operate inside the broken domain. Cannot repair $\Sigma_D$ from outside $\Sigma_D$.
Step 2Stabilization:  $t \geq \tau_{\text{lock}}$  coherence maintained under decoherence pressure
WhyTransient coupling is insufficient. $\psi_D$ must persist long enough to satisfy the coherence constraint. Duration is not arbitrary — it is determined by the system.
Step 3Release:  $\psi_D \to \Psi_F$  latent energy $\Delta E$ discharged at transition
WhyStabilization accumulates potential across the substrate boundary. Release converts it into the coupling update.
Step 4Update:  $C_0 \to C_1$  coupling architecture permanently modified
WhyThis is the point of irreversibility. The old coupling constant is replaced. The system cannot return to $C_0$.
Step 5Propagation:  $C_1 \otimes \Sigma_D$  new coupling available across derivative space
WhyThe update is not local. $C_1$ propagates to all entangled subsystems in $\Sigma_D$. Activation still requires $Q = 1$ (Section χ).
Irreversiblecost $\to \infty$ in volume limit
Observable$\Delta C$ visible only in cross-substrate processes
χ
Activation Constraint (Global Gate)

Everything above converges here. Ten factors, one integral, one gate. The coupling architecture (Section 10) makes $C_1$ available. But availability is not activation. The gate $Q$ — will — is binary. Zero shuts down the entire functional regardless of what the other nine factors contain. The system is offered. The observer chooses.

Gate$Q = 0 \Rightarrow \chi = 0$   ·   $Q \neq 0 \Rightarrow \chi > 0$ possible
InterpretCoupling available $\forall Y$, activated iff $Q = 1$
