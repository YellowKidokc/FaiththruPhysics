Books I through III derive requirements from information theory, thermodynamics, Gödel, Chaitin, Shannon, and Kolmogorov. At no point in those derivations does any religious idea appear. VIII. The Probability Calculation

How likely is it that any given worldview satisfies all constraints by accident?

We have 8 independent boundary conditions. Call the probability of any arbitrary worldview satisfying a single boundary condition p. If we assign a generous p = 0.5 (coin flip—any worldview is equally likely to satisfy or fail any given constraint), the probability of satisfying all 8 independently is:

P(all 8) = p⁸ = 0.5⁸ = 0.0039 = 0.39%

But p = 0.5 is absurdly generous. Most worldviews do not affirm necessary existence. Most do not affirm mathematical-moral unity. A more realistic estimate might be p = 0.2:

P(all 8) = 0.2⁸ = 2.56 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.000256%

And if we use all 20 axioms rather than the 8 boundary conditions:

P(all 20) = 0.5²⁰ = 9.54 × 10⁻⁷ ≈ 1 in a million
P(all 20) = 0.2²⁰ = 1.05 × 10⁻¹⁴ ≈ 1 in 100 trillion

These are lower bounds. The actual probabilities are likely much smaller, because the constraints are not truly independent—satisfying some limits which others can be satisfied, making the combined probability even smaller than the product suggests.
