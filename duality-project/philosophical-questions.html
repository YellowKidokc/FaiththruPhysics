The Opening Test

The project begins with the hardest possible prompt: find the world where evil wins permanently. The simulations do not start by defending good. They start by giving evil every chance to prove it can stand alone.

Can evil exist without good?

The null scenario strips the world down until evil has nothing left to corrupt, exploit, or devour.

Answer: evil is parasitic. Total victory removes the substrate it needs.

Does good need evil to define itself?

The pure-good scenario tests whether goodness becomes meaningless without opposition.

Answer: good is generative. It does not require evil for identity or motion.

Questions That Carry the Story

Origin

  1. How does evil emerge from perfect good?
  2. Is free will worth the risk of evil?
  3. Can perfection be corrupted from within?

Decay

  1. Is balance between good and evil stable?
  2. Do evil choices compound over generations?
  3. Can corrupted systems self-correct?

Rescue

  1. What is required to fix a broken system?
  2. How can sacrifice become victory?
  3. Can death be reversed rather than escaped?

Destination

  1. Can ambiguity exist forever?
  2. Is hell destruction or isolation?
  3. Does being simulated diminish meaning?
